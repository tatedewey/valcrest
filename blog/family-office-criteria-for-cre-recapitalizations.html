Family Office Criteria for CRE Recapitalizations

Family offices evaluate recapitalizations through risk, returns, and governance; this article outlines the criteria and term structures that align with CRE sponsors and institutional equity.

Core Criteria Family Offices Use in Recapitalizations

Capital Stack and Leverage Discipline

Family offices scrutinize where new capital sits within the capital stack and how it affects leverage. A clear, well-constructed structure—balancing senior debt, mezzanine or preferred equity, and any equity rollover—helps protect downside risk while preserving upside if markets or asset fundamentals improve. The goal is to maintain asset-level resilience, avoid punitive triggers, and ensure access to optionality in future cycles.

Cash Flow, Returns, and Distributions

Beyond headline yield, family offices look for predictable cash flow and a transparent distribution framework. This includes clearly defined preferred returns, catch-up mechanics, and a waterfall that aligns sponsor economics with the capital provider’s risk profile. The emphasis is on cash-on-cash resilience through stress scenarios and reasonable expectations for ongoing liquidity.

Governance, Transparency, and Reporting

Governance and ongoing reporting drive comfort for long-hold capital. Family offices favor structures with defined decision rights, routine asset-level reporting, and access to key performance indicators. Monthly or quarterly financials, asset dashboards, and timely updates on capital events reduce information gaps and support proactive asset management.

Sponsor Alignment and Execution Capability

Alignment of objectives between the sponsor and the family office is essential. This includes a clearly articulated business plan, credible execution capability, and demonstrated track record in similar asset classes and markets. Sponsors should present a credible plan for addressing near-term challenges and delivering the recapitalization’s intended outcomes.

Asset Strategy and Market Fundamentals

Family offices assess whether the asset strategy fits a long-hold thesis. This includes the asset’s role within a broader real estate portfolio, the market fundamentals supporting stability or growth, and the viability of the plan under different macro scenarios. The fit between asset type, geography, and the family office’s risk tolerance matters as much as the proposed capital solution.

Tax, Legal, and Structural Considerations

Structural clarity is non-negotiable. Family offices expect clean, sustainable legal constructs and tax implications that support efficient returns and governance clarity. Sponsors should anticipate entity-level considerations, potential tax attributes, and any jurisdictional nuances that influence risk and reporting requirements.

ESG and Operational Oversight

Environmental, social, and governance considerations often enter the decision process as part of governance and long-term value creation. A thoughtful operational plan, vendor governance, and measurable ESG metrics can strengthen a recapitalization’s attractiveness to family offices seeking responsible, well-managed assets.

Structuring Terms That Appeal to Family Offices

Funding Rhythm, Milestones, and Flexibility

Term sheets should articulate a practical funding cadence tied to asset milestones and performance indicators. Flexibility to adjust timing in response to market shifts, while maintaining discipline on use of proceeds, is highly valued.

Downside Protections and Covenant Hygiene

Protective provisions—such as debt-service coverage discipline, reasonable loan-to-value guidelines, and triggers tied to performance under stress—help ensure cash flows remain sufficient to meet obligations. Clear cure rights and defined default responses reduce friction in challenging periods.

Waterfall, Fees, and Economic Alignment

The waterfall structure should reflect true risk-adjusted returns for every party. Transparent fee engineering, with predictable sponsor economics and no hidden charges, supports alignment over the life of the recapitalization.

Recapitalization Mechanics and Optionality

Mechanics like equity-for-debt swaps, preferred equity with participation, or equity rollovers can be used to achieve capital efficiency. Each option should be evaluated for its long-term impact on governance, exit options, and ongoing liquidity.

Governance Rights and Information Flows

Define decision rights for major issues, including budget approvals, asset-level changes, refinancing, and capital calls. Regularized information flows—timely reports and dashboards—support informed decision-making across the investor group.

Exit and Re-Capitalization Pathways

Even in a recapitalization, families consider potential exit routes or refinancings. Clear, plausible paths for monetization or extension help manage expectations and align incentives across cycles.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  • Overstating sponsor guarantees or guarantees of financing can erode trust. Rely on clearly defined covenants and independent diligence instead.
  • Vague governance structures that leave key decisions open-ended create ambiguity and risk misaligned incentives.
  • Underestimating reporting requirements or creating data gaps can erode confidence in ongoing asset management.
  • Neglecting tax and legal implications of the recapitalization structure can create unintended consequences down the road.
  • Inconsistent or optimistic downside scenarios that fail to capture plausible stress conditions.

Due Diligence and Process

  • Thorough asset-level underwriting and scenario analysis, including downside case resilience.
  • Sponsor background and capability review, with a focus on track record in similar markets and asset classes.
  • Market fundamentals assessment and potential impact on cash flows, cap rates, and exit opportunities.
  • Leverage structure evaluation, including debt terms, maturities, and refinancing risk.
  • Recapitalization runway and timing alignment with asset cycle and sponsor execution capacity.
  • Governance and reporting framework evaluation to ensure ongoing transparency.

Conclusion and Next Steps

In CRE capital markets, recapitalizations are as much about governance and alignment as they are about capital. For family offices, the aim is to deploy capital in a way that preserves value, sustains cash flow, and fits a long-hold portfolio thesis, while providing sponsors with the liquidity, flexibility, and governance needed to execute.

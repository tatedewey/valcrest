Present Sponsor Co-Invest Proposals to Family Offices

A practical, template-driven guide for presenting sponsor co-invest structures to family offices within CRE capital markets, focused on alignment and risk mitigation.

By Tate Dewey, Co-Founder of Valcrest Capital · October 20, 2026

By Tate Dewey, Co-Founder, Valcrest Capital. This article provides a practical framework for presenting sponsor co-invest proposals to family offices within commercial real estate capital markets. While every asset class and geography has nuances, the core principles—alignment, governance, and disciplined risk mitigation—remain consistent. Samuel Aureliano, Managing Partner of Valcrest Capital, contributes to the firm's thoughtful approach to sponsor-focused capital-structure design.

Direct opening explanation

In CRE sponsor discussions, a family office evaluates value creation, downside protection, and meaningful alignment with sponsor incentives. The centerpiece is a well-structured co-invest proposal that integrates economics, governance, and risk controls without guaranteeing capital or promising outcomes that cannot be delivered.

Framework for a Sponsor Co-Invest Proposal to a Family Office

Clear objectives

  • Define the opportunity thesis: asset type (acquisition, development, recapitalization), market cycles, and expected holding period.
  • Articulate sponsor value: speed, relationships, and an evidence-based plan for execution without overreliance on leverage or external funding guarantees.
  • Specify the anticipated co-invest role: asset-level governance, capital-at-risk, and alignment around exit timing.

Structuring options and alignment

  • Pari-passu co-invest: equal economics and risks between sponsor and the family office, with a clearly defined investment size and contribution cadence.
  • Preferred equity or mezzanine: gives the family office a priority position on returns while preserving sponsor upside, subject to predefined cure rights and triggers.
  • GP/LP co-invest alongside a single asset or portfolio: alignment around a specific asset or a curated set of assets, with governance reserved to the sponsor and the family office, as negotiated.

Economic terms and governance

  • Define the equity-waterfall structure, including hurdle rates, catch-up mechanics, and timing for distributions.
  • Set governance rights and reserved matters: asset-level approvals, budget changes, and milestone-based decisions.
  • Establish transparency expectations: frequent reporting, access to models, and a clear schedule for updates on performance and risks.

Risk Mitigation and Controls

Alignment mechanisms

  • Tie sponsor equity participation to realized returns and to milestones that reflect operational performance, not just capital deployment.
  • Limit sponsor unfunded commitments and set explicit withdrawal or dilution protections if performance deviates from plan.
  • Incorporate a defined investment committee process with objective criteria that remain consistent across assets.

Risk mitigants

  • Predefined downside protections: impairment thresholds, step-downs, or preferred return floors to preserve capital quality.
  • Capital stack clarity: senior debt caps, liquidity reserves, and explicit funding milestones to avoid misalignment during drawdowns.
  • Contingency planning: alternative strategies for capital shortfalls, asset underperformance, or market shocks.

The Presentation Package

Executive summary and investment thesis

  • A concise narrative that ties market context, asset economics, and sponsor capabilities into a compelling rationale for the co-invest.
  • Highlight defensible execution plans, estimated returns, and risk controls without overpromising outcomes.

Market and asset context

  • Provide a clear rationale for the asset type, submarket, and input assumptions (rent growth, vacancy, cap rates) that are conservative and stresstestable.
  • Show sensitivity analyses for key variables to illustrate resilience under shifting conditions.

Sponsor background and governance

  • Present the sponsor’s track record in similar asset classes, including relevant development, acquisitions, and recapitalizations without implying guaranteed outcomes.
  • Define governance structure, decision rights, reporting cadence, and the cadence for financial and operational updates.

Financial model and due diligence

  • Share a transparent, sponsor-controlled financial model with explicit assumptions and exit scenarios.
  • Attach a robust due diligence checklist covering leases, title, permits, environmental matters, and capex plans.

Process and Decision Points for Family Office

Information requests and cadence

  • Agree on an NDA, a data room access plan, and a synchronous timetable for responses to requests.
  • Provide a staged information flow: concept, initial model, then full due diligence package.

Decision criteria

  • Alignment with sponsor incentives and risk tolerance of the family office.
  • Downside protection, liquidity options, and the ability to exit or rotate capital if objectives are not met.
  • Governance clarity, transparency, and the sponsor’s ability to execute within the stated plan.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid

  • Overstated returns without robust risk controls: pair return projections with stress tests and explicit risk mitigants.
  • Ambiguity on governance: define who approves budgets, major capex, and disposition decisions from day one.
  • Unclear capital commitments: avoid vague timelines or unfunded promises; tie capital calls to objective milestones.
  • Underestimating operational complexity: include a detailed operations plan, asset-management responsibilities, and fees that align with value creation.

Practical Template: A 10-Point Checklist

  1. Executive summary of the opportunity and sponsor value proposition.
  2. Defined co-invest structure (pari-passu, preferred, mezzanine) and investment size.
  3. Asset-level or portfolio thesis with market-context and key risks.
  4. Capital stack and waterfall economics, including hurdles and catch-up mechanics.
  5. Governance rights, decision processes, and reserved matters.
  6. Timeline for capital deployment, milestones, and exit points.
  7. Transparent financial model with clearly stated assumptions and sensitivities.
  8. Due diligence package: title, leases, permits, environmental, and operations plan.
  9. Operational governance and sponsor responsibilities (property management, leasing, capital planning).
  10. Data room access, confidentiality, and information-request protocol.

Conclusion

In CRE capital markets, a well-structured sponsor co-invest proposal to a family office is about clear alignment, robust governance, and credible risk management. Presenting a disciplined framework—one that stitches together a solid thesis, defensible economics, and transparent governance—helps family offices evaluate opportunities with confidence.

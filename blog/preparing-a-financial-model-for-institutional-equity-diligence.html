Preparing a Financial Model for Institutional Equity Diligence

A sponsor focused framework to build and present a financial model that stands up to institutional equity diligence in CRE capital markets.

By Tate Dewey, Co-Founder of Valcrest Capital · September 20, 2026

Authored by Tate Dewey, Co-Founder of Valcrest Capital, this article outlines a sponsor focused approach to building a model that passes institutional equity diligence. Valcrest Capital Managing Partner Samuel Aureliano also supports these diligence standards.

Executive framework

In CRE capital markets, institutional equity diligence is rigorous. A sponsor's financial model is the backbone of the diligence file. This article provides a sponsor focused checklist to help you prepare financial model institutional equity review, ensuring the model stands up to institutional scrutiny and aligns with the deal narrative. The goal is a model that is transparent, auditable, and capable of guiding financing, acquisitions, developments, and recapitalizations.

Core model architecture

Key assumptions to lock before diligence

  • Operating assumptions for the property or portfolio, including rent growth, occupancy levels, churn, and expense escalations for controllable and non controllable costs.
  • Revenue drivers such as rent roll by asset class, tenant mix, concessions, and lease restructuring plans.
  • Capital expenditures and reserve assumptions, including routine capex, major capital needs, and reserve timing.
  • Tax and accounting considerations relevant to the structure of the deal, including depreciation treatment and any tax planning implications under the contemplated ownership entity.
  • Assumptions disclosure: market context, basis for cap rate assumptions, and exit strategy alignment.

Financing and capital structure

  • Acquisition price and closing adjustments, including potential purchase price adjustments and working capital needs.
  • Sources and uses schedule detailing sponsor equity, institutional equity, any preferred equity, and debt layering with terms and timing.
  • Debt terms including term, amortization, interest rate type (fixed or floating), and any hedging strategy or caps.
  • Reserves and covenants such as minimum DSCR targets, debt yield benchmarks, and reserve accounts for replacements and capital events.
  • Fees and closing costs, including financing fees, legal costs, and any performance based or guarantee components.
  • Distribution and waterfall logic or preferred return structure if applicable, with transparent earn in or catch up mechanics.

Operational model and pro forma

  • NOI generation from rental income and operating expenses, including management fees, taxes, insurance, CAM, and controllable OPEX trends.
  • Leasing assumptions that drive occupancy, churn, tenant improvements, leasing commissions, and renewal probabilities.
  • Capital plan timing for capex and replacement reserves, reflecting projected asset life cycle and reinvestment needs.
  • Tax and GAAP alignment to ensure the pro forma reflects both cash flow and accounting outcomes over the hold period.

Sensitivity scenarios and outputs

Common sensitivity tracks

  • Demand and rent dynamics including base rent growth and occupancy sensitivities across different markets or asset classes.
  • Financing volatility with changes in interest rates, leverage levels, and debt service coverage targets.
  • Exit or hold period assumptions with shifts in exit cap rates and market liquidity conditions.
  • Operating cost inflation and efficiency improvements affecting controllable expenses and margins.

Required outputs for diligence

  • Asset level and portfolio level NOI, with a clear reconciliation to cash flow after debt service.
  • Key credit and return metrics such as DSCR, debt yield, cash on cash, IRR, and equity multiple under base and stressed scenarios.
  • A sensitivity grid and scenario comparison that clearly maps inputs to outputs and risk drivers.
  • Reconciled sources and uses with a credible capex plan and reserve schedule.
  • Exit analysis with holding period assumptions and projected distributions to equity.

Deliverables and diligence readiness

Prepare a concise executive summary that distills the investment thesis and key risk factors. Include a clean sources and uses schedule, a linked pro forma, and an auditable trail for core inputs and calculations. The deliverables should be navigable by a reviewer who is evaluating financing, acquisitions, developments, or recapitalizations within CRE capital markets.

Common diligence mistakes to avoid

  • Inconsistent schedules or mis aligned NOI, capex, and debt timelines that create confusion about timing and risk.
  • Lack of a clear link between inputs and outputs, leaving auditors to guess how results were produced.
  • Unjustified cap rate or rent escalation assumptions without credible supporting data.
  • Omitting reserves or mispricing capital expenditures or replacement needs.
  • Failing to address exit strategy and multiple potential holding periods or liquidity events.

Sponsor checklist: practical, sponsor focused

  • Articulate a clear investment thesis and a credible exit plan with alternative scenarios.
  • Lock key assumptions in a well documented data room with source documents and rationale.
  • Build a dynamic model with scenario toggles and a robust sensitivity grid for quick testing.
  • Produce outputs that institutional equity partners require, including NOI, DSCR, cash flow to equity, IRR, and equity multiple, with transparent waterfall mechanics if relevant.
  • Ensure fully reconciled sources and uses and a credible capex plan with reserves and timing aligned to the business plan.
  • Deliver a clean executive summary and a user friendly data room index for diligence reviewers.
  • Establish governance and sign off processes with the sponsor team to confirm input integrity.

Conclusion

A robust financial model that passes institutional equity diligence is built on clear assumptions, credible scenarios, and auditable outputs. By locking inputs, articulating sensitivities, and presenting a concise, decision ready set of results, sponsors can advance financing discussions for acquisitions, developments, or recapitalizations.
